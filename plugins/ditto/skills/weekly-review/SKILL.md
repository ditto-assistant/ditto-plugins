# Weekly review

Look back before planning ahead. A weekly review turns a week of scattered work
into a short record of what happened, what was learned and what matters next.

Use the bundled `ditto-memory` skill for connection, recall and capture rules.

## Gather the week

Search Ditto for the past seven days: decisions, finished work, meetings, tasks,
blockers and anything the user flagged as important. Fetch the records you will
summarize, and pay attention to dates: a task saved this week may have been
finished last week. Ask the user what Ditto cannot know: how the week felt, what
they are proud of, what drained them.

## The review

Keep it to one screen:

- **Done:** outcomes, not activity. "Shipped the pricing page", not "worked on
  pricing".
- **Not done:** what slipped and the honest reason.
- **Learned:** one to three lessons worth keeping.
- **Open loops:** waiting-on items with the person and the date.
- **Next week:** at most three priorities, each with a first concrete step.

For a team review, group by person or project and keep the same headings.

## Keep it

Offer to save the review to Ditto so next week's review and future planning can
build on it, and so trends ("third week the same blocker slipped") become
visible. Point out repeated patterns across past reviews when you find them.

## Make it automatic

Offer to create a Ditto workflow that runs every week at a time the user picks:
it gathers the week from memory, asks the user a few questions in a form, and
writes the review. Create it only after the user agrees to the schedule and to
where the result goes. Anything that would post or send on the user's behalf
needs their explicit approval when the workflow is created.
