# Prepare for a meeting

Walk in knowing what was said last time, what is still open, and what you want
out of this meeting. Walk out with the decisions and action items saved.

Use the bundled `ditto-memory` skill for connection, recall and capture rules.

## Before: the brief

Ask for the meeting's topic, attendees and goal if they are not given, or read
them from a connected calendar when the user points you at the event. Then
search Ditto for:

- each attendee and their company or team: role, what they care about, past
  conversations and commitments made to or by them;
- the project's latest decisions, open questions and blockers;
- promises from the previous meeting and whether they were kept.

Fetch the memories you rely on. Use web search only for public facts (a company's
recent news), and never put private memory details in a public query.

Deliver one page: purpose and desired outcome, who is attending and what to know
about each, history in three to five bullets, open items, and suggested
questions or an agenda. Mark anything inferred, and note any gap ("no record of
the pricing discussion").

## After: capture

When the user shares notes, a transcript or a quick recap, extract decisions,
action items (owner and due date), open questions and notable facts about the
people. Show them for a quick check, then save on the user's yes as one meeting
record, linked to the project and people it concerns. Use the `decision-log`
skill for decisions the team will need to find later.

Sending a recap to attendees, creating calendar events or assigning tasks in
another tool are separate actions: draft them, and act only when asked.

## Make it routine

Offer a Ditto workflow that builds this brief automatically before recurring
meetings. Set it up only when the user agrees to the schedule and to anything it
would send.
